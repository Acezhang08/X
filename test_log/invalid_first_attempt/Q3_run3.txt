**1904 Olympic marathon (St. Louis, Aug 30, 1904)**
- Thomas Hicks (USA) won in 3:28:53 after being helped by trainers who gave him strychnine and brandy.
- Fred Lorz crossed the line first in about 3:13 but was disqualified, because he had ridden roughly 11 miles in a car.
- Only 14 of the ~32 starters finished. It was about 90°F (32°C), with a single water stop around mile 12.
- Cuban postman Félix Carvajal finished 4th after stopping to eat apples along the way.
- Len Taunyane and Jan Mashiani, two Tswana runners, finished 9th and 12th. They were the first Black Africans to compete at the Olympics.

**Q1: Jan Ernst Matzeliger**
- He was born Sept 15, 1852 in Paramaribo, Dutch Guiana (now Suriname), and died Aug 24, 1889 in Lynn, Massachusetts, aged 36, of tuberculosis.
- He moved to the US around 1876 and settled in Lynn, Massachusetts in 1877, where he worked in shoe factories.
- He patented his shoe-lasting machine on March 20, 1883 (US Patent 274,207).
- The machine could last 150–700 pairs of shoes a day, against about 50 by hand.
- The Consolidated Lasting Machine Company bought the rights, and the business later became part of United Shoe Machinery.

**Q4: Elizabeth Magie**
- She was born May 9, 1866 in Macomb, Illinois, and died Jan 2, 1948.
- She invented The Landlord's Game and patented it on Jan 5, 1904 (US Patent 748,626).
- She was a follower of Henry George and designed the game to show how land monopoly harms tenants.
- The game had two rule sets: "Prosperity", where wealth is shared, and "Monopoly", where players try to ruin each other.
- In 1935 Parker Brothers bought her patent for $500 and credited Charles Darrow as Monopoly's inventor.
