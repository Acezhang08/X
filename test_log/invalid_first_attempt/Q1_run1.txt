## Q1: Jan Ernst Matzeliger
- Born 15 Sept 1852 in Paramaribo, Dutch Guiana (now Suriname). His father was Dutch and his mother was a Surinamese woman.
- He moved to Lynn, Massachusetts, in 1877. He worked in shoe factories and taught himself English.
- He patented his shoe "lasting machine" on 20 Mar 1883 (US Patent 274,207).
- The machine could last about 150–700 pairs of shoes a day. A skilled hand laster managed about 50.
- He died of tuberculosis on 24 Aug 1889, aged 36. The US issued a stamp honoring him in 1991.

## Q4: Elizabeth Magie
- Born 9 May 1866 in Macomb, Illinois. She died on 2 Jan 1948.
- She invented The Landlord's Game and patented it on 5 Jan 1904 (US Patent 748,626).
- She was a Georgist, and the game was meant to show how land monopoly harms tenants and to promote a single land tax. It had two rule sets: one monopolistic and one cooperative.
- It spread through word of mouth and was the basis for Monopoly. Charles Darrow patented his version in 1935.
- Parker Brothers bought her patent in 1935 for $500, with no royalties. Her role was largely forgotten for decades.
