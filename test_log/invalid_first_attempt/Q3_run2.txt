**1904 Olympic marathon (Q3 repeats the opening question, so one answer covers both)**
- **Date and place:** 30 August 1904, St. Louis, in about 90°F (32°C) heat, over roughly 40 km of dusty, hilly roads. There was only one water stop, at about 12 miles.
- **Winner:** Thomas Hicks (USA) won in 3:28:53. His trainers gave him strychnine mixed with egg white and brandy to keep him going.
- **Disqualification:** Fred Lorz reached the finish first, in about 3:13, but had ridden in a car for roughly 11 miles. He was disqualified.
- **Field:** About 32 men started and only 14 finished. Albert Corey was second (3:34:52) and Arthur Newton third (3:47:33).
- **Notable runners:** Cuban postman Félix Carvajal ran in street clothes with his trousers cut short and finished fourth. Len Taunyane and Jan Mashiani, Tswana runners, were the first Africans at the Olympics. Taunyane finished ninth.

**Q1: Jan Ernst Matzeliger**
- Born 15 September 1852 in Paramaribo, Dutch Guiana (now Suriname), to a Dutch father and an enslaved Surinamese mother.
- Left Suriname as a sailor at about 19 and settled in Lynn, Massachusetts, around 1877. He worked in shoe factories while teaching himself English.
- Patented his shoe lasting machine on 20 March 1883 (US Patent 274,207). It attached the upper to the sole mechanically.
- His machine produced about 150–700 pairs of shoes a day, against about 50 by hand. It cut shoe costs sharply.
- Died of tuberculosis on 24 August 1889, aged 36. The US Postal Service issued a stamp in his honor in 1991.

**Q4: Elizabeth Magie**
- Born 9 May 1866 in Macomb, Illinois. She died 2 January 1948.
- Patented "The Landlord's Game" on 5 January 1904 (US Patent 748,626).
- She designed it to teach Henry George's land-value-tax ideas. It had two rule sets: one rewarding monopolists and one rewarding shared prosperity.
- Variants of her game spread through the 1900s–1920s, and Charles Darrow later sold a version as Monopoly.
- In 1935 Parker Brothers bought her patent for $500 and gave her no royalties. She was also a writer, stenographer and stage performer.
