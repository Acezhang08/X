## Q1: Jan Ernst Matzeliger
- Born 15 Sept 1852 in Paramaribo, Dutch Guiana (now Suriname). He emigrated and settled in Lynn, Massachusetts, in 1877.
- He worked in the shoe factories of Lynn, then the center of US shoemaking. He spent about 6 years developing a machine for "lasting", which attaches the upper part of a shoe to the sole.
- He patented the lasting machine on 20 March 1883 (US Patent 274,207).
- The machine made 150–700 pairs of shoes a day, against about 50 by hand. This is often credited with cutting shoe costs by roughly half.
- He sold the rights to the Consolidated Lasting Machine Company. He died of tuberculosis on 24 Aug 1889, aged 36.

## Q3: 1904 Olympic marathon
- It was held on 30 Aug 1904 in St. Louis, Missouri, in about 90°F (32°C) heat. The 24.85-mile course was hilly and dusty, with only one water stop, at about 12 miles. Only 14 of 32 starters finished.
- **Winner:** American Thomas Hicks, in 3:28:53. His handlers gave him strychnine and brandy during the race, and he nearly collapsed at the finish.
- **Fred Lorz** crossed the line first, but he had ridden about 11 miles in a car after cramping. He was disqualified once he was found out, though he had claimed it was a joke. He later won the 1905 Boston Marathon.
- **Felix Carvajal**, a Cuban postman, finished 4th. He raced in street clothes and stopped to chat with spectators and eat apples, some of which were reportedly rotten.
- **Len Taunyane and Jan Mashiani**, two Tswana men from South Africa, finished 9th and 12th. They were the first Africans to compete in an Olympic marathon. Albert Corey (USA) took second.

## Q4: Elizabeth (Lizzie) Magie
- Born 9 May 1866 in Macomb, Illinois. She was a stenographer, writer, actress and political activist, and she died in 1948.
- She invented **The Landlord's Game** and patented it on 5 Jan 1904 (US Patent 748,626).
- She designed it to show how land monopolies harm tenants, based on the single-tax ideas of economist Henry George. It had a looping board with properties, rents and a "Go to Jail" square, and it had two rule sets: one monopolistic and one prosperity-sharing.
- The game spread informally for about 30 years, including to college campuses. Charles Darrow then sold a version of it to Parker Brothers as **Monopoly** in 1935.
- Parker Brothers bought Magie's patent for $500 in 1935, with no royalties. Darrow was long credited as sole inventor and became a millionaire, while Magie's role was largely forgotten until recent decades.
