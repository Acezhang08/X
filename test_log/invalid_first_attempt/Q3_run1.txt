**1904 Olympic marathon (Q3 repeats the first question, so one answer covers both)**
- **Date and conditions:** It was held on Aug 30, 1904, in St. Louis, in about 90°F heat on dusty roads. The course had only one water station, at the 12-mile mark.
- **Field:** About 32 runners started and only 14 finished.
- **Winner:** Thomas Hicks (USA) won in 3:28:53. His trainers gave him strychnine mixed with egg whites and brandy during the race.
- **Cheating:** Fred Lorz crossed the line first, but he had ridden in a car for about 11 miles. He was disqualified and later won the 1905 Boston Marathon.
- **Other runners:**
  - Albert Corey was 2nd (3:34:52) and Arthur Newton was 3rd.
  - Felix Carvajal, a Cuban postman, finished 4th after stopping to eat apples.
  - Len Taunyane and Jan Mashiani, who were Tswana, were the first Africans to compete in an Olympic marathon.

**Q1: Jan Ernst Matzeliger**
- He was born Sept 15, 1852, in Paramaribo, Dutch Guiana (now Suriname). His father was Dutch and his mother was Surinamese.
- He left home at about 19 to work as a sailor and settled in Lynn, Massachusetts, in 1877. He worked in the shoe factories there.
- He patented his shoe "lasting" machine on March 20, 1883 (US Patent 274,207).
- The machine could make 150–700 pairs of shoes a day, against about 50 by hand.
- He died of tuberculosis on Aug 24, 1889, aged 36. The US Postal Service issued a stamp honoring him in 1991.

**Q4: Elizabeth Magie**
- She was born May 9, 1866, in Macomb, Illinois, and died March 2, 1948. She worked as a stenographer, writer, actress and comedian.
- She invented The Landlord's Game and patented it on Jan 5, 1904 (US Patent 748,626).
- She was a follower of Henry George and designed the game to show how land monopolies harm tenants. The game had two rule sets, one monopolistic and one cooperative ("Prosperity").
- It spread through word of mouth, and its mechanics influenced the later game Monopoly. She received a second patent in 1924.
- Parker Brothers bought her patent for $500 in 1935, with no royalties. At the same time it began selling Monopoly, which it credited to Charles Darrow.
