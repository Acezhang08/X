**Q1: Jan Ernst Matzeliger**
- Born 15 Sept 1852 in Paramaribo, Dutch Guiana (now Suriname). His father was Dutch and his mother was Surinamese.
- He emigrated to the US in 1876 and settled in Lynn, Massachusetts, the centre of the shoe industry, around 1877.
- He invented a shoe-lasting machine, which attaches the upper part of a shoe to the sole. He received US Patent 274,207 on 20 Mar 1883.
- Hand lasters finished about 50 pairs a day. His machine did roughly 150–700 pairs a day, cutting shoe costs sharply.
- He died of tuberculosis on 24 Aug 1889, aged 36. His patents went to the Consolidated Lasting Machine Company.

**Q4 (same as the first question): Elizabeth Magie**
- Born 9 May 1866 in Macomb, Illinois. She was a stenographer, writer and feminist who followed Henry George's land-tax ideas.
- She patented "The Landlord's Game" as US Patent 748,626, filed in 1903 and granted 5 Jan 1904.
- The game was meant to show how land monopolies enrich landlords and impoverish tenants. It had two rule sets, anti-monopolist and monopolist.
- It spread informally and influenced Monopoly. In 1935 Parker Brothers bought Charles Darrow's version, then bought Magie's patent for $500 with no royalties.
- She renewed her patent in 1924 and died on 2 Mar 1948 in Arlington, Virginia. She received little credit for the game in her lifetime.
