## Q4 / Elizabeth Magie
(Same as the first question, so I've answered it once.)
- **Born** May 9, 1866 in Macomb, Illinois. She died January 2, 1948. She was a writer, stenographer, actress and Georgist, a follower of Henry George's land-tax ideas.
- **Invented** The Landlord's Game, a board game meant to show how land monopolies enrich landlords and impoverish tenants.
- **Patent** She filed in 1903, and US Patent 748,626 was granted January 5, 1904. She patented a revised version in 1924.
- **Two rule sets** The game had a "monopolist" version, where players win by bankrupting others, and a "prosperity" version, where wealth is shared.
- **Monopoly link** Parker Brothers bought her patent for $500 in 1935, the same year they began selling Charles Darrow's Monopoly. She received no royalties, and her role was widely forgotten for decades.

## Q1 / Jan Ernst Matzeliger
- **Born** September 15, 1852 in Paramaribo, Dutch Guiana (now Suriname). His father was a Dutch engineer and his mother was Surinamese.
- **Moved** to Lynn, Massachusetts, a shoemaking center, in 1876, and worked in a shoe factory.
- **Invention** A shoe "lasting" machine, which pulls the leather upper over the shoe's form and attaches it to the sole. It took him roughly six years to develop.
- **Patent** US Patent 274,207, granted March 20, 1883. The machine could last about 150–700 pairs a day, against about 50 by hand.
- **Died** August 24, 1889, aged 36, of tuberculosis. His patent rights had been sold to the Consolidated Lasting Machine Company. The US Postal Service issued a stamp in his honor in 1991.
