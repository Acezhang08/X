## Q1: Jan Ernst Matzeliger
- Born Sept 15, 1852, in Paramaribo, Dutch Guiana (now Suriname). He died Aug 24, 1889, aged 36, of tuberculosis.
- Moved to the US around 1876 and settled in Lynn, Massachusetts, in 1877. He worked in a shoe factory.
- Patented his shoe lasting machine (US Patent 274,207) on March 20, 1883.
- The machine made 150–700 pairs of shoes a day, against about 50 by hand lasting.
- The Consolidated Lasting Machine Company bought the patent. The machine cut shoe production costs sharply and helped make New England's shoe industry dominant.

- **Price:** $599 list, or $499 after a $100 mail-in rebate.
- **Cancelled:** Sept 4, 2007, announced by Palm CEO Ed Colligan. It never shipped, with launch only weeks away.
- **Specs (from memory, so treat these as approximate):**
  - 10.2" display at 1024×600.

## Q3: 1904 Olympic marathon
- Held Aug 30, 1904, in St. Louis, in heat of about 32°C (90°F) and thick road dust. It was run over about 40 km.
- 32 runners started and only 14 finished. The course had just one water station, at about 12 miles.

## Q4: Elizabeth Magie
- Born May 9, 1866, in Macomb, Illinois. She died Jan 2, 1948.
- She invented The Landlord's Game and received US Patent 748,626 for it on Jan 5, 1904.
- She designed it to show how land monopolies enrich landlords at tenants' expense, drawing on Henry George's single-tax ideas. Her game had two rule sets, one anti-monopolist and one monopolist.
- The game spread informally through the 1900s–1930s and evolved into versions that led to Monopoly.
- In 1935 Parker Brothers bought her patent for $500 with no royalties, after Charles Darrow's Monopoly became a hit. Darrow was long credited as the sole inventor.
