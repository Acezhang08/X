**Q1: Jan Ernst Matzeliger**
- Born 15 Sept 1852 in Paramaribo, Dutch Guiana (now Suriname), to a Dutch engineer father and a Surinamese mother.
- Emigrated to the US around 1876 and settled in Lynn, Massachusetts, in 1877. He worked in a shoe factory.
- Invented a shoe-lasting machine, patented 29 Mar 1883 (US Patent No. 274,207). It attached the upper to the sole mechanically.
- The machine could produce 150–700 pairs of shoes a day. Hand lasters managed about 50.
- Died of tuberculosis on 24 Aug 1889, aged 36. His patents were bought by the Consolidated Lasting Machine Company.

**Q4: Elizabeth Magie**
- Born 9 May 1866 in Macomb, Illinois. She died 2 Jan 1948.
- She invented The Landlord's Game and patented it on 5 Jan 1904 (US Patent No. 748,626).
- It was meant to show the injustice of land monopolies, based on Henry George's ideas. It had two rule sets: one monopolist and one anti-monopolist.
- The game spread through word of mouth and became the basis for Monopoly. Charles Darrow sold his version to Parker Brothers in 1935.
- Parker Brothers bought Magie's patent for $500 in 1935, with no royalties. She was largely forgotten until the 1970s.
